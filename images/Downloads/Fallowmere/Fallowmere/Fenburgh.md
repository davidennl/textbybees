_A lakeside town of 8,000 souls, caught between church authority and guild independence, now frozen under a fairy's curse._

---
## Geography

Fenburgh sits on a **gentle slope** rising from the Fallowmere's shore. The town is divided into three districts:

**Lower Fenburgh (The Harbour)**: Guild territory. Warehouses, workshops, fisher cottages, the pearl divers' lodge, weaving halls. Narrow streets, salt-stained timber buildings, the smell of fish and damp wool. The **Songstress Tavern** ("The Winking Seal") sits near the docks—neutral ground with about twenty rooms.

**Middle Fenburgh**: The transition zone. Craftsmen, shopkeeps, minor clergy, and the town market. Most daily life happens here. The **goblin market entrance** is hidden somewhere in this district—perhaps in an abandoned cellar or behind a false wall in a storage shed.

**Upper Fenburgh (Cathedral Hill)**: The Cathedral of the Blind Judge dominates the summit. Surrounding it are living quarters for the faithful, a handful of craftsmen (millers, weavers, bakers), two apothecaries (one run by a Justiciar priest), and a printing press (also church-run). This is not a wealthy district—just modest stone houses and the practical infrastructure of a devout community.

**The West Wall**: A low stone wall faces the Grim Mire. Four watchtowers. The main gate is barred and chained. Sentinels patrol in pairs (eight on duty at any time, rotating shifts).

**Fenburgh Beach**: A sandy stretch along the lake's edge where children catch sweet herring, crabs, and freshwater oysters during warmer months. Now frozen and silent, the beach is empty except for ice fishermen trying to bore through the curse-hardened surface.

---
## The Curse
For three months, the harbor has been frozen solid. Any ship that attempts to break through the ice and sail into the lake **vanishes**—no wreckage, no survivors. Two vessels have been lost so far (one fishing boat, one merchant ferry).

**The cause**: **Harker**, a desperate fisherman, has been secretly hunting Fell Seals. He's killed three in the past two months. Abenay, the Patron Fairy of the Fallowmere, cursed Fenburgh in response. The fairy considers the seals sacred wards of the lake.

**Consequences**:
- Fishing boats are stuck. Ferries can't leave. Trade has stopped.
- The Weaver's Guild can't export their goods.
- Food supplies are dwindling. The church controls rationing from the cathedral grain stores.
- Tensions are rising. People are looking for someone to blame—fairborn, witches, fairy collaborators.

## Key Locations
### 1. The Cathedral of the Blind Judge

Upper Fenburgh, highest point. Grey stone, simple stained glass with geometric patterns. The bell rings three times daily—dawn, noon, dusk.

**Mother Wilhelmine**: Early sixties, grey hair in a tight bun, sharp eyes. She's pragmatic and devout. She genuinely believes the curse is the Reaper's punishment for Fenburgh's tolerance of fairy influence. She doesn't want to burn anyone, but she will if she believes it's necessary to save the town. She's frustrated—the curse persists despite her efforts, and she suspects someone in Fenburgh is still trafficking with fairies.

**The cathedral cellar**: Grain stores, rationed by the church. One small cell for prisoners awaiting trial.

**The Justiciar services**: The church runs two essential community services on Cathedral Hill:

- **The Apothecary of Saint Merciful** (run by **Brother Clement**, a kind priest in his fifties who treats the sick and dispenses medicine)
- **The Fenburgh Press** (run by **Sister Avila**, a stern woman in her forties who prints church pamphlets, guild notices, and the occasional broadsheet of town news)

These services ensure the church remains embedded in daily life, not just a distant authority.

---
### 2. The Sentinel Barracks
Adjacent to the cathedral. Stone building, two stories, functional. Houses **30 Sentinels** total—enough to patrol, guard key locations, and respond to threats, but not enough to control the town by force alone.

**The Lady's Lance Erikr**: Commander of the Sentinels. Late forties, tall, cropped grey hair, missing two fingers on his left hand (old injury). Pragmatic and disciplined. He respects Wilhelmine but doesn't defer to her automatically. Their relationship is professional but tense—she sees the Sentinels as her enforcers; he sees them as servants of the Reaper, not the church hierarchy.

**Erikr's position**: He knows the burnings aren't solving anything. He suspects fairy smuggling but has no proof. He's loyal to the Reaper, not to Wilhelmine personally. If the party can demonstrate a better path, he might quietly support them.

**The barracks arsenal**: Secondary weapons storage—spears, crossbows, light armor. Guarded but accessible to Sentinels on duty.

---
### 3. Pyre Square
Middle Fenburgh, town square. A raised stone platform with a single iron post. Blackened wood at the base.

**The burnings**: Two in the past century. The first was 80 years ago (a witch accused of bargaining with the Grim Mire's fairy). The second was 40 years ago (a magician caught with sprite-flasks). Both times, the accused were guilty by Justiciar law.

**Now**: Tensions are rising. The curse has persisted for months. People are scared and looking for someone to blame. Whispers of fairborn, witches, fairy collaborators circulate. Wilhelmine hasn't ordered a third burning yet, but the pressure is building. If she doesn't act soon, the town might act for her.

---
### 4. The Winking Seal Tavern (Songstress Outpost)
Lower Fenburgh, near the harbor. Three stories, timber construction. Common room seats about forty. Second floor has six private rooms. Third floor is the Songstress quarters (three residents).

**Madame Voss**: Runs the outpost. Early forties, dark hair, shrewd eyes. A skilled spy and information broker. Reports to Redstone Keep (the queen monitors Fenburgh's church) and to the Blind Hand (they pay for information and assistance with fairy shipments). Not evil—amoral. A job is a job. If the party offers better leverage or coin, she'll consider switching sides.

**Current role**: She knows about the fairy smuggling operation (she facilitates communication between the Fisherman's Guild faction and the Blind Hand). She also suspects someone triggered Abenay's curse but doesn't know who. She hasn't told Wilhelmine because that's not her job—yet.

---
### 5. The Feast (Harbor Inn)
Lower Fenburgh, right on the docks. Two stories, timber and stone. Ground floor is a low-ceilinged common room with a massive fieldstone hearth. Smells of fish stew, ale, woodsmoke. This is where the Fisherman's Guild gathers after shifts.

**The atmosphere**: Loud, rough, working-class. The food is simple but filling. The ale is cheap. Fights break out occasionally, but the innkeeper (**Old Torsten**, a former fisherman with one eye and a booming voice) keeps order with a cudgel and a sharp tongue.

**Who's here**: Fishermen, dockworkers, a few pearl divers. This is where guild politics happen informally—arguments, deals, gossip. If the party wants to learn what the guilds are thinking, this is the place.

---
### 6. The Guild Halls
Lower Fenburgh, near the harbor.

#### Fishermen's Guild Hall
Largest building. Timber, two stories. Common room downstairs (smells of fish, tar, salt). Meeting room upstairs. The guild has **~400 members**—half the harbor's working population.

**Internal division**:
- Some members are devout Justiciars who attend cathedral services and support Wilhelmine.
- Others resent the church's rationing and power.
- A few are secretly fairborn or sympathetic to fairies.

**The smugglers**: A small faction (6-8 members, led by **Jens**, a gruff and desperate man) has been catching fairies at night and selling them to the Blind Hand via Madame Voss. They use nets and iron cages. Since the curse began, they can't deliver their shipments to the Queen's Isle. The Blind Hand is pressuring them to find another route or break the curse. The smugglers are furious and increasingly desperate.

**The seal hunter**: **Harker**, one fisherman, has been hunting Fell Seals in secret. His family is starving, and seal pelts are valuable. He's killed three seals in the past two months. He doesn't know his actions triggered Abenay's curse. He's wracked with guilt but too scared to confess publicly (he'll be executed).

---
#### Pearl Divers' Guild Lodge
Stone building, always damp. The guild has **~30 members**—small, dangerous work.

**Tolerance**: The divers don't care about Justiciar doctrine. They care about survival. One of their best divers—**Oswin**—has blue-grey hair, which he usually keeps covered with hoods or scarves. Blue-grey hair is unique to Fenburgh, always associated with proximity to the Fallowmere or Grim Mire. It's rare (maybe 1 in 500 births). Most fairborn hide it. Oswin is careful—he doesn't advertise his nature, but the guild quietly protects him because he's the best diver. He can dive deeper and hold his breath longer than anyone else.

**Tensions**: With the curse and rising paranoia, Oswin is increasingly vulnerable. Someone recently left a bundle of kindling and a Justiciar prayer card on his doorstep—a threat.

---
#### Weaver's Hall
Timber building, two stories. Looms on the ground floor, offices upstairs. The guild has **~150 members**—second largest.

**Wealth**: The weavers turn pearls into "river silk" embroidery—Fenburgh's primary export. They're the wealthiest guild, but the curse has cut them off from trade routes. They're losing money fast.

**Resentment**: They blame the church for the curse (claiming Wilhelmine's rigidity provoked the fairies). They're furious about the rationing—they can afford to buy food, but the church won't let them.

**Outside connections**: The weavers have contacts in Helmstadt, Redstone Keep, and Imperial cities. If anyone in Fenburgh knows what's happening in the wider world, it's them.

**Guild head**: **Margit**, a sharp-eyed woman in her fifties who's increasingly frustrated with the church's control, and who has ambitions of her own.

---
### 7. The Church Dock & The Galley
North end of the harbour. Stone pier, iron gate. Two Sentinels guard the gate at all times.

**The Galley - "Silverflight"**: A war galley. 100 feet long, two masts, reinforced hull. Black paint, the Judge's blindfold on the sail. Crew of 25 sailors (Justiciar faithful), plus 10 Sentinels when deployed.

**Why it matters**:

- **Arsenal**: Largest weapons cache in Fenburgh—crossbows, spears, oil barrels, armor. Controlling the ship means controlling military capacity.
- **Mobility**: The only vessel that can break through ice. Control means controlling trade routes and escape.
- **Symbol**: Represents church authority. Its presence keeps the guilds in check.

**Current status**: Frozen in. Two feet of harbor ice surrounds it. The crew could break through (takes a full day of preparation—chipping ice, firing up the oars), but Abenay's curse means any ship that sails vanishes.

**Access**: Church officials and Sentinels only. The guilds can't use the dock, which fuels resentment.

---
### 8. The Frozen Harbor
Ice covers the water. Fishing boats are stuck. The guild docks are silent.

**The vanishings**: Two ships tried to break through and sail to Helmstadt. Both vanished—no wreckage, no survivors. No one tries anymore.

**Mood**: The harbour is silent. Fishermen mend nets they can't use. Divers sit idle. The weavers have no pearls to work with. Desperation is building.

---
### 9. Fenburgh Beach
A sandy stretch along the lake's edge. During warmer months, children catch sweet herring, crabs, and freshwater oysters here. Families gather for picnics. It's one of the few places in Fenburgh that feels carefree.

**Now**: Frozen solid. The beach is empty except for a few desperate ice fishermen trying to bore through the curse-hardened surface.

---
### 10. The Goblin Market
**Location**: Hidden entrance in Middle Fenburgh—perhaps in an abandoned cellar, beneath the floor of a public well, or behind a false wall in a storage shed. Goblins leave subtle markers for those who know how to look (scratched symbols on walls, specific arrangements of stones).

**The Market itself**: Underground. A warren of tunnels and rooms carved into the earth over decades. The main chamber is thirty feet across, with stalls and trade goods. Side tunnels lead to storage, workshops, and living quarters.

**The tragedy**: One year ago, the goblins dug too deep. They broke through into the **Deep Roads**. A creature came up through the breach—grey-skinned, elongated limbs, moving on all fours. It killed 40 goblins** in the first hour (two thirds of the market's population). All-Father Gorb rallied the survivors, and they collapsed the tunnel entrance, sealing the breach. The creature didn't follow—as far as they know.

**Current state**:
- The market is vast but feels empty. Only 20 goblins remain** (down from 60).
- The deep section is abandoned. The collapsed tunnel is visible—rubble and hastily reinforced supports. Goblins avoid even looking at it.
- **10 goblin children** remain, all under Gorb's care.

**All-Father Gorb**: An older goblin, wrinkled and stooped, with bright eyes and a patient demeanor. He's the caretaker for the market's children—the highest honor in goblin culture. He's haunted by the loss. He blames himself for the breach. He still tends the youngsters, but he's quieter now, more cautious.

**The other goblins**: Maybe 10 adults. They trade, dig cautiously (never deep), and steal from Fenburgh at night (small things—forgotten tools, discarded fabric, dropped coins). They're not malicious, just opportunistic. They avoid the cathedral and Sentinels. They know Fenburgh is dangerous right now.

**What they know**: The goblins hear everything through the walls. They know about the smuggling, the seal hunting, the tensions between church and guilds. They know the cathedral cellar has a prisoner (they can hear through the stone). They know where the fairy smugglers meet. They won't volunteer this information, but they'll trade for it.

**What they want**: Safety. The curse has everyone on edge, and goblins are always scapegoated. If Fenburgh descends into witch hunts, the market will be discovered and destroyed. Gorb wants the curse lifted and stability restored.

---
### 11. The West Wall & Gates
Stone wall, ten feet high. Four watchtowers. The main gate faces the Grim Mire road (barred and chained).

**Patrols**: Sentinels walk the wall in pairs (eight on duty at any time, rotating shifts). They watch for Horrors, but also for anyone trying to leave without permission.

**The Mire beyond**: Frozen marshland. Leafless trees. Fog that never lifts. Everyone knows Ainslinn, the Patron Fairy of Decay, and her captive Horror (Lodewick the Fair) are out there. No one wants to go looking.

**Patrols sent**: Wilhelmine has sent three patrols into the Mire (each 4-6 Sentinels) to investigate Ainslinn's tower. None returned. Erikr thinks it's a waste of lives.
