**Locations**
- _The Marsh’s Edge_  
    The ground grows treacherous and slick, every step sending ripples across shallow pools that seem to appear from nowhere. The air is heavy with the musk of peat and rot. Bran catches a figure in the distance—something half-seen through the drifting mist. A Perception check (DC15) will reveal whether he truly glimpses the Horror he has hunted, or merely its shadow.

Progress through the marsh is perilous. The party must attempt a group Stealth check (DC15). On a failure, a ring of pale-winged fairies descends, shrieking with laughter as they torment the trespassers. Each player suffers one level of exhaustion as the fey drain warmth and willpower alike.

Further on, the mire deepens. Quicksand lies hidden under a thin veil of reeds and moss. The party must succeed collectively on an Athletics check (DC15), or be dragged beneath. Once caught, escape requires a Strength check (DC15). Three failed attempts means suffocation and death beneath the bog.

The ground slopes upward at last, where a tangle of gnarled trees claws against the sky. On one twisted bough sits _Pale Mist_, a sorrowful fairy cloaked in silver light, her eyes like milky pools. She speaks in riddles, hinting at the Horror’s origins and Bertrade’s fate. Hoofprints appear in the mud below, twinned by human bootprints beside them. Pale Mist offers a vision, but demands a toll: each player must surrender something precious—an heirloom, a secret, or a cherished memory.

Arcana or Investigation checks (DC15) here reveal flashes of the truth: Bertrade’s final battle, the unholy pact that bound her, and the despair that still taints the marsh.

- _The Heart of the Marsh_  
    The climb ends abruptly. Before you lies a hollow at the crown of the hill, a drowned barrow where stagnant water has gathered into a black, motionless pool. The air here is a suffocating miasma—methane thick as oil, a stench of sulfur and decay that stings the eyes. The sky itself seems heavy, as though pressing down upon the earth.

No birds sing. The few trees that rise from the water are pale and skeletal, their bark sloughing away in strips like rotting skin. A hush holds the marsh, broken only by the gurgle of bubbles rising from the black water.

Then you see it. Standing waist-deep in the bog, still as a grave marker, is [[The Bog Horse]]. Gaunt, its flesh hangs like drenched cloth, a rotted saddlebag half sunk into the exposed spine. Around it three ghouls lurch clumsily, occasionally falling into the mud, their skin bruised and leatherlike.
