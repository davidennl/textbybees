"The Long Night" is built a welcome sight at the best of times, but during Reaping Season its a sanctuary worth its weight in gold. Built for defence, not comfort, It’s a two-story, timber-and-stone building that looks more like a small fortified waystation than a cosy inn. The ground-floor windows are narrow, barred slits. A few goats wander in a pen around the back of the building, and a statue of the Lord Reaper stands in the yard. Arcana DC10 demonstrates the statue is imbued with a faltering hallow spell.

- **The Common Room (Ground Floor):**
    - This is the main room. The ceiling is low, supported by thick, dark wooden beams. The air is warm and smells of woodsmoke, ale, and damp wool. A massive **fieldstone hearth** dominates one wall, fire blazing within it. This is the main source of light and heat, and the inn's patrons have arranged their tables around it. The Innskeep, Boris, a broad-shouldered man with a quiet, gruff demeanor, works behind a bar of solid oak thick enough to stop a charging bull.
    - There's a painting of a battle above the fireplace. It depicts embellished soldiers in green and silver (Fallowmere) fighting against faceless Imperial legions. A DC12 Perception check reveals an old woman amongst the melee. She wears black plate and an ivory lance. 
        
- **Upstairs:**
    - A single, creaking wooden staircase leads upstairs.
    - **Private Rooms:** There are **four private rooms**. They are small and sparse: a sturdy bed with a straw mattress, a wool blanket, a small washbasin, and a wooden chest. The doors are heavy and have simple iron bar-locks on the inside. These are for the merchants or wealthier refugees. Each have a little effigy over the door: an image of the Lord Reaper, except in his hands he holds not a blindfold and weighing scales, but an ebony kite shield.
    - **Common Sleeping Room:** At the end of the hall is a large, unheated attic room. It contains **ten straw pallets** on the floor. This is the cheap option for the down-on-their-luck travellers. A single, small, barred window looks out over the road.

- **The Cellar & Secret Basement:**
    - **Larder:** A heavy wooden door behind the bar leads down to a small, damp, stone-walled cellar. It’s filled with barrels of ale and mead, sacks of root vegetables, and hanging cured meats.
         The innskeep is protective of this area. If the players get a chance to look, a **DC 15 Investigation** check reveals one of the large, heavy kegs of mead is set against the stone wall in a way that _seems_ impractical, blocking a small alcove.
    - **The Passage:** If the keg is rolled aside (a **DC14 Strength** check), it reveals a section of the wall that is not mortared stone, but tightly-fitted, dark timber.
        There's no obvious lock. Instead, a **DC16 Investigation** check on the timber wall reveals a small, thumb-sized knot in the wood that is perfectly smooth. It's a plug. Pulling it out reveals a keyhole.
    - **The Armory:** Unlocking and pushing this panel reveals a narrow, dark, earth-smelling tunnel that descends into a hidden sub-basement. This room is dry and organized. Racks hold simple spears, arming swords, and crossbows. A few open chests contain boiled leather armour, chain shirts, bundles of bolts, and whetstones.

### Patrons of "The Long Night"
The common room is quiet, holding only a few groups.

- **The Merchants:** A table of three men, two look like hired guards, the third a merchant in a fur-lined cloak. They are drinking heavily and speaking in low, anxious tones about the Imperial Road and rumours of "Horrors" there.
    
- **The Refugees:** A husband and wife, dressed in fine clothes that are now soiled from travel, sit in a dark corner. They don't speak, watching their two young children, who are asleep on a bench seat. They nurse a single cup of wine between them and flinch when the inn door opens.
    
- **The Down-on-their-Luck Traveller:** A single, elderly man in the ragged robes of a pilgrim (perhaps to a Widow Convent, or some other shrine). He sits by the fire, eating a bowl of stew the Inskeep gave him. He has a bed in the common sleeping room and is coughing badly.

- **Sir Lucia**: a familiar face, Sir Lucia fought a wyvern alongside Mae a decade or so back. Lucia is happy to see Mae alive, and reminisces about their time together. She recently lost her apprentice, Squire Jean, to two undead on the Imperial Road. One was a scout, the other an old woman pretending to be a refugee. Sir Lucia followed them along the Imperial road for a while, but decided she was more needed at the Long Night, which is being harassed by undead coming from nearby villages at night.
	- Sir Lucia will vouch for Mae to the innskeep.
	- She is also willing to trade up to 2gp at 80sp/1gp
